## The Book

**Title:** _The Way of Excellence_
**Author:** Brad Stulberg
**Finished:** August 2026
**Rating:** ★★★★★

## One-paragraph summary

A field guide to sustaining excellence over a lifetime rather than chasing it in bursts. Stulberg defines excellence as *involved engagement in something worthwhile that supports your values and goals* — a combination of **mastery** (getting better at something) and **mattering** (it being connected to what you actually care about). Explicitly, excellence is **not** perfectionism, obsession, optimization, happiness, or flow. Part 1 grounds this in biology, psychology, and philosophy; Part 2 turns it into sixteen practical mindsets and habits — from care and goals to rest, community, and completion.

## Key takeaways

1. **Excellence = mastery + mattering.** It's a process of becoming, not a destination — measured by whether a pursuit is aligned with your values, not by external markers of success.
2. **Three needs drive sustained motivation:** autonomy, competence, and belonging. Any goal or project is worth testing against whether it increases or decreases these.
3. **Consistency beats intensity.** "Raise the floor" on bad days instead of chasing peak days, keep a short memory for both wins and losses, and let progress be a by-product of consistently showing up rather than the goal itself.
4. **Drop "balance" — build an identity house instead.** Devote most of your time and energy to a few rooms (pursuits) rather than spreading it evenly across everything; keep the rest at a minimum effective dose so the whole house doesn't rest on one pillar.
5. **Rest is part of the work, not a break from it.** *Stress plus rest equals growth.* Genuine rest requires no self-control, no anxiety, and not turning recovery into another task to optimize.

### How the book is organized

**Part 1 — Foundations**
- The Biology of Excellence
- The Psychology of Excellence
- The Philosophy of Excellence

**Part 2 — Mindsets, Habits, and Practice** (16 chapters): Care, Goals, Consistency, Trade-Offs, Focus, Discipline, Renewal, Confidence, Patience, Routine, Gumption, Curiosity, Failure, Community, Joy, Completion.

Each chapter pairs research (psychology, neuroscience, philosophy) with a short, concrete set of practices — closer to a workbook than a straight narrative.

### A few practices worth pulling out

- **Deep-focus blocks:** 50 minutes to 2 hours of single-tasking, followed by a 5–30 minute break (a walk, not a scroll).
- **Daily / weekly / monthly rhythm:** the book suggests picking a few non-negotiables at each timescale — e.g. daily movement + one deep-focus block, weekly outdoor walks + time with people, monthly reflection + a day mostly outdoors — rather than copying someone else's routine wholesale.
- **The 48-hour rule:** give yourself up to 48 hours to sit with a big win or loss, then get back to the work.
- **Completion rituals:** mark milestones deliberately (a dinner, a note in a box) instead of drifting from one goal straight into the next.

## Who should read it

Good for anyone chasing long-term achievement in a craft, sport, or career who's tired of hustle-culture advice that burns out in a month. It reads well alongside Stulberg's earlier books (_Peak Performance_, _The Passion Paradox_) and will feel most useful to people who want research-backed reasoning *and* a workbook-style set of practices to actually apply, not just theory. Because it covers a lot of ground (19 chapters), it's less of a quick read and more of a reference to revisit chapter by chapter.
